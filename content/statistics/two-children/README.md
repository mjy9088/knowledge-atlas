# two-children draft reader

## Explanatory purpose and scene plan

The reader should leave able to construct the observation denominator, not merely remember that two versions have answers 1/3 and 1/2. The central distinction is between a true family property and the procedure that produces a report of that property. No quiz or random experiment is required to obtain the explanation. The [scene plan](slide-plan.json) and the representation rationale below are authoring proposals, not evidence of learner attainment or editorial approval.

| Scene | Content and purpose | Representation and meaningful interaction | Success check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `question` | The same true sentence can arrive through different procedures; identify what information is missing before calculating. | Static question/process comparison, without an answer reveal or required prediction. | Reader can ask how the family or child was selected rather than choose an answer from wording alone. |
| `model` | Declare independent equiprobable B/G outcomes and birth-order identities; explain why BG and GB are separate cases. | Static four-prior figure only: no report procedure has been introduced, so do not display a conditioning ledger or its result yet. | Reader can reconstruct four weights of 1/4 without treating the model as demographic evidence. |
| `filter` | Work truthful family questioning: BB, BG and GB each retain mass 1/4; normalize by 3/4. | The model-backed ledger aligns prior × report likelihood = retained mass, then shows the summed denominator and normalized target. Sampling is optional and secondary. | Reader derives (1/4)/(3/4)=1/3 and explains why an unspecified boy does not identify a fixed child. |
| `child` | Work uniform family then uniform child selection: enumerate the four surviving outcomes from eight equiprobable outcomes; connect their multiplicity to masses 1/4, 1/8, 1/8. | The same fixed rows expose BG/GB likelihoods changing from 1 to 1/2; canonical counting remains visible, and selected-procedure feedback explicitly identifies any difference from this scene's authored case. | Reader derives 2/4=(1/4)/(1/2)=1/2 and explains why BG and GB lose half their original weight. |
| `missing` | Define a, b and c as reporting probabilities for BB, BG and GB; derive a/(a+b+c), conditional on a positive denominator and truthful reports. | Static unknown-policy diagram plus the conditional formula; do not install an arbitrary reporting policy as an interactive default. | Reader explains why the truth of the report excludes GG but does not determine the other reporting weights. |
| `transfer` | Change the question to whether the firstborn is a boy; retain BB and BG, exclude GB, and justify the second child's probability. | An original four-case diagram identifies the fixed coordinate and the surviving numerator/denominator. This is worked exposition, not a compulsory quiz. | Reader derives 1/2 for a different reason and states when the intuitive independent-other-child argument is valid. |

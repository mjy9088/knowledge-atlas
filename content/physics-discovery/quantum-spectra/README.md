# Quantum spectra episode

This episode moves from four selected NIST atomic-hydrogen Balmer transitions with rounded air wavelengths to individual-line photon energy differences and keeps Bohr's hydrogen success separate from modern quantum states. It treats `E=hν` as the exact one-photon relation and uses the same-frequency phase-index relation `λvac=n_airλair` before obtaining `410 nm ≈ 3.02 eV` versus `656 nm ≈ 1.89 eV` with the explicit `n_air≈1` approximation. NIST §17 equation (13) grounds local line emissivity, not complete observed brightness; M1/E2 “forbidden” radiative transitions are not erased. ## Decisive source passages reopened for this revision

The registered NIST §19 live page was opened on 2026-10-19. Equation (30) gives the nonrelativistic hydrogenic transition energy. In “Some transitions of the main spectral series of hydrogen,” rows `2-3` through `2-6` give 6562.80, 4861.32, 4340.46, and 4101.73 Å; footnote b says wavelengths above 2000 Å are in air. The page also identifies lower/upper principal shell numbers in footnote c. This supports the four selected rounded air positions and their hydrogenic transition assignments. It does not support a claim that excited hydrogen emits only these four lines, a low-pressure condition, absence of continuum radiation, measured brightness, line widths, or uncertainties.

The registered NIST §17 live page was also opened. The selection-rule table lists E1 as “allowed” and M1/E2 as “forbidden,” while the same page gives nonzero conversion relations for common M1/E2 transitions. Equation (13), `εline=(4π)⁻¹hνAkiNk`, defines local radiant power per source volume per solid angle: `Aki` is a transition rate in `s⁻¹`, and `Nk` is the upper-level excited-atom number density in `m⁻³`. Complete observed brightness additionally depends on optical depth, source geometry, and instrument response. Equation (14) reaches radiance only for a homogeneous optically thin source of path length `l`. Equation (25), `λ̄vac=nλ̄air=hc/ΔĒ`, is explicitly a weighted multiplet relation and is not cited as an unbarred individual-line equation.

NIST §15 states that standard-air wavelengths are often tabulated above 200 nm and are related to level differences by conversion to corresponding vacuum wavenumbers or frequencies. For an individual line, `λvac=n_airλair` is therefore presented here as the same-frequency phase-index deduction, not as the literal scope of §17 equation (25). OpenStax supplies the individual-photon `|ΔE|=hν=hc/λvac` relation.

The registered OpenStax §6.2 passages beginning “The energy absorbed or emitted” give `|ΔE|=hν=hc/λ`, derive the hydrogen energy relation, report agreement with the Rydberg constant, and state that the model cannot extend correctly to helium and retains untenable precise classical orbits. The approximate `E_n≈−13.6 eV/n²` calculation is reported only to matching precision: about 656 nm for `3→2` and 410 nm for `6→2` in vacuum. The NIST table values 656.3 and 410.2 nm are rounded air wavelengths, so the figure presents correspondence rather than exact equality. Section 6.3 opens by distinguishing hydrogen success from failure for helium/larger atoms; “The Quantum–Mechanical Model of an Atom” represents the electron state with the wavefunction `ψ` and interprets `|ψ|²` as position probability density. No OpenStax prose, figure, or orbit artwork is imported.

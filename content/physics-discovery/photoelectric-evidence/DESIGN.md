# Photoelectric-evidence figure design contract

## 1. Scope and direction

This contract extends the shared paper-and-ink reader with seven original static schematics. The signature relationship is a controlled comparison: frequency sets photon energy and the emission threshold; at fixed above-threshold frequency, intensity changes arrival rate and may change saturation-current magnitude without changing maximum electron energy or stopping-potential magnitude. Apparatus, qualitative observations, the single-photon model, and regime limits remain visibly distinct.

The figures are explanatory geometry, not copied OpenStax curves, measured data, fitted models, historical apparatus reconstructions, simulations, photographs, or scale drawings. Any current–voltage curve is explicitly qualitative and schematic, with no invented error bars or numerical current ratio. Current means photocurrent magnitude, avoiding a false identification of electron travel direction with conventional-current sign.

All seven stable scene IDs and their order, the four claim IDs, worked-block ranges `[0,2)` and `[2,end)`, `needs-content-review`, planned 180-second narration, and `review-required` status remain unchanged. Static figures add no quiz, topic adapter, hidden state, audio-fit claim, or learner-attainment claim.

## 2. Source-grounded representation boundary

The registered OpenStax §6.2 page was opened before production. Its apparatus paragraphs establish an evacuated tube, illuminated emitting photoelectrode, collector, ammeter, variable electrode potential, and reversed stopping potential. Figure 6.9's discussion establishes different saturation-current plateaus at two intensities and the same stopping potential; equation (6.12) and Example 6.6 distinguish a negative retarding collector voltage from its positive magnitude. The cutoff subsection establishes no ordinary photocurrent below a material cutoff. Equations (6.13)–(6.15) establish `Eγ=hf`, `Kmax=hf−φ`, and `fc=φ/h`.

Those relationships inform independently authored figures only. No source prose, figure geometry, curve coordinates, data, activity, or assessment is reproduced. Broad threshold language is limited here to the clean, uniform metal, vacuum, monochromatic, low-intensity single-photon regime already recorded by the claims. The existing Farkas exception is retained without adding a new claim: sufficiently strong laser fields may permit multiphoton or strong-field emission.

## 4. Figure primitives

- **Complete apparatus circuit:** the emitter and collector are metal electrodes connected to two named circuit nodes. An adjustable DC bias source lies across those nodes. The ammeter is in series in the collector-current path; the voltmeter is a separate parallel branch across the same two electrode nodes. Every wire visibly terminates at a node/electrode/meter/source terminal. Meter labels sit in clear component interiors rather than under wires. Under generic retarding bias the collector is labelled negative relative to the emitter without borrowing the worked example's `−1.0088 V`; the electric field points right and the force on a right-moving negative electron points left.
- **Root meter component:** a meter is a labelled circular component with two explicit terminals and a semantic role (`ammeter-series` or `voltmeter-parallel`). Visible wire segments stop at the terminal gaps and never run through a meter or source symbol; separate component metadata records electrical continuity through each symbol. The ammeter's two terminals belong to the single collector loop. The voltmeter's two wire branches terminate directly at the emitter and collector electrode nodes, before the ammeter. Tests reconstruct visible wire reachability from coordinates and compare it with the separate component-terminal metadata; metadata alone is insufficient.
- **Qualitative I–V axes:** horizontal voltage is collector relative to emitter; vertical quantity is photocurrent magnitude. Two same-frequency curves share one screen-coordinate stopping intercept and have vertically ordered saturation plateaus. Physical current ratios and generic voltage values are not encoded. They are labelled “schematic, not data,” with no points, error bars, or unique quantitative model.
- **Three-case energy ledger:** photon energy `hf` is compared directly with work function `φ`. For `hf<φ`, emission is blocked and no negative kinetic-energy bar or electron arrow appears. At exact threshold `hf=φ`, the ideal maximum energy is `Kmax=0`; this necessary energy equality does not guarantee measurable current or receive an emission arrow. Only the authored `hf>φ` branch has positive `Kmax` and an explicitly conditional emitted-electron arrow; the figure does not claim one incident photon guarantees one collected electron.
- **Simple continuous-accumulation comparator:** a concise, visibly labelled authored hypothesis says that if one electron simply accumulated wave energy continuously, increasing brightness should help overcome the shortfall. The adjacent observation says that below cutoff no ordinary photocurrent appears within the stated regime. This is the narrow OpenStax classical-discussion comparison actually consulted, not a claim that one experiment refuted every classical theory under all conditions.
- **Matched-condition strip:** fixed surface, frequency, collection geometry, and adequate collection voltage stay visible while intensity changes. One continuous, noncountable flow cue per condition labels photon arrival rate `lower → higher`, while equal-size text chips label saturation-current magnitude relationally; no repeated marks, bar-length comparison, or numeric arrival/current ratio is encoded. `hf`, `Kmax`, and `|Vs|` retain their worked-example values and visible eV/V units.
- **Scope boundary:** low-intensity single-photon inference is visibly bounded; multiphoton/strong-field processes sit outside it. Two separate visible statements say the result neither refutes all classical theories under all conditions nor proves all quantum mechanics.

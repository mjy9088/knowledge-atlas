# Nuclear scattering episode

## Explanatory contract

The article treats the angular distribution as evidence rather than displaying a nucleus as though it were observed. The apparatus scene distinguishes early 1907–1909 angle/thickness/back-scattering work from the refined 1912–1913 rotatable microscope-screen example and establishes the thin-foil and angular-acceptance controls. The pattern scene compares a diffuse-charge account with a concentrated-center account while preserving multiple scattering as something constrained by foil thickness, not wished away. The inference scene distinguishes the angular pattern's support for charge concentration from additional hints and later work relevant to adoption of a positive center; it does not claim that scattering angles alone distinguish the sign or independently establish mass concentration. The following scene separates 1909 observation, 1911 modeling, later tests, and later particle physics.

## Evidence and limits

The canonical OpenStax and AIP URLs were opened again on 2026-10-19 before figure production. In OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* §2.2, the paragraph beginning “The next major development” describes the high-speed positive alpha beam, thin gold foil, and luminescent screen; the paragraph beginning “What did they discover?” and Figure 2.9's caption give the qualitative mostly-through / some-slightly-diverted / very-few-significantly-deflected pattern. The deduction list and Figure 2.10 discuss a small positive massive nucleus and the large region where the probe is not strongly deflected. This revision uses those passages only as a maintained textbook account; it does not import their prose, figures, quotation, simulation, numerical proportions, mass inference, or literal “empty space” wording.

In the AIP exhibit, the paragraph beginning “Rutherford and Hans Geiger worked closely” and the following paragraph beginning “A year later in Manchester” describe 1907–1908 detection work and zinc-sulfide scintillations counted through a microscope at different dispersion angles. The passage from “The autumn of 1908 began an important series of researches” through the paragraph beginning “Geiger and Marsden began with small-angle dispersion” describes foil work and thickness questions; the paragraphs beginning “Sometime later in 1908 or 1909” and “Human memory is fallible” bound the backward-scattering report and May 1909 submission. The paragraphs beginning “What was Rutherford doing for the rest of 1909 and all of 1910?”, “In the autumn of 1910”, and “At some point in the winter of 1910–1911” describe gradual model development rather than an instant conclusion.

For the inference boundary, the paragraph beginning “In fact, Rutherford was exceedingly cautious” supplies the intense-field inference and thickness, charge-squared, and inverse-fourth-power speed predictions. The paragraph beginning “Rutherford entertained the possibility that the charged center is negative” says the central-charge sign did not affect the scattering calculations. The next paragraph beginning “Rutherford did see possible tests of the nature of the central charge” identifies beta-particle absorption and alpha-particle emission velocity as possible sign tests, then explicitly ends “But these were only hints.” The figure-caption paragraph beginning “This schematic represents the refined experiments of 1912–13” identifies one microscope-screen assembly rotatable around the foil; the following paragraph beginning “Geiger and Marsden did indeed work systematically” describes the 1912 and 1913 tests and reports a historian's suggestion that Rutherford decided for a positive center by August 1912. These are consulted passage locators, not imported quotations in the Korean article or proof that angles determined the sign. They support apparatus chronology, cautious charge-concentration inference, separate hints about sign, and distinct observation/model/test stages; they do not supply measured trajectories, a complete research history, or an inevitable route to modern particle physics.

“Mostly empty” is therefore qualified as low probability of strong deflection for this probe, not literal vacuum or absent electrons. Thin foil limits multiple-scattering contributions but does not prove that every event was a single encounter. The acceptance comparison is conceptual coverage in the depicted scattering plane, not a perfect all-solid-angle or 4π detector. The episode does not infer neutrons, quantum orbitals, protons’ internal structure, or quarks from gold-foil scattering.

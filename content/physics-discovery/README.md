# Physics discovery short series

## Editorial purpose

This local-review series asks how a physical claim became warranted: what was changed or measured, what pattern appeared, what inference the pattern supported, and what remained unresolved. Ten Korean articles move from ordinary motion through atomic, nuclear, quantum, relativistic, and quark evidence without presenting history as a predetermined march toward current theory. Each page uses the shared static reader. The series ID is `series:physics-discovery`; `DISCOVERY_ENTRIES` from `catalog.ts` is included in the canonical content catalog, the canonical references live in `sources/registry.json`, and the corresponding concepts live in `knowledge/concepts.json`.

The intended reader knows everyday motion and basic ideas of charge but need not know calculus, statistical mechanics, or quantum theory. Vocabulary appears only when it does explanatory work. Each article preserves the limits of its own experiment or model: nuclear scattering does not establish quarks, a light-clock deduction is not itself an experiment, and a charge ledger does not count every instantaneous proton constituent.

The other articles record their decisive passages and exclusions in their topic READMEs, with canonical identities in `sources/registry.json`. Exact immutable bytes and provider revisions were not captured unless a record explicitly says otherwise; null hashes and pending rights review are preserved. The Korean prose, cards, questions, and comparisons are independently authored. No source figure, quotation, simulation, dataset, or code is included.

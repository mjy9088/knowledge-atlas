# Quark-evidence episode

## Explanatory contract

This candidate answers one question: which measured and processed deep-inelastic-scattering pattern supported charged point-like constituents in the proton? It separates one event's `E′` and `θ` from the ensemble differential cross section; available beam energy from positive event momentum-transfer scale `Q²=-qμqμ`; hadronic invariant mass `W` from structure functions `W₁` and `W₂`; and observation from parton-model inference. The early 6° and 10° spectra did not separately determine `W₁` and `W₂`, so estimates of `W₂` depended on model-extraction extremes for `R=σL/σT`, not a measured uncertainty band.

The hard-center interpretation contrasts coherent scattering from an extended charge distribution, which is form-factor suppressed at increasing resolution, with incoherent scattering from unresolved independent charged centers. The schematic never assigns a constituent count. The Nobel retrospective reports that the parton interpretation was later identified with the quark model; this reader does not derive a complete quark identity from the proton pattern alone. Electrically neutral gluons are partons but not direct photon charge-scattering centers; their role enters here only through separately sourced `Q²`-dependent scaling violations and later evidence. The episode does not cover `uud` bookkeeping, sea content, or constituent counting; those form the independent `proton-charge-ledger` candidate.

## Passages actually read

- **Nobel Prize 1990 press release and illustrated presentation:** “The prizewinners’ contributions” gives the 4–21 GeV beams, spectrometer angles, measured scattered-electron energy, and unexpectedly frequent large-angle/large-energy-loss events. “The SLAC–MIT experiment” says the charge appeared concentrated in negligible-size components and that the parton interpretation was later identified with the quark model. The presentation's simplified collision image is not treated as a literal photograph.
- **Breidenbach et al., SLAC-PUB-650 (1969), INSPIRE scan, abstract and printed pp. 2–6 consulted:** printed p. 2 compares the Mott-divided cross section against the paper's positive `q²` at **constant hadronic invariant mass `W`**, defines `W²=2M(E−E′)+M²−q²`, and says the deep-continuum dependence is weaker than elastic/resonance behavior. Printed p. 3 gives `(d²σ/dΩdE′)/(dσ/dΩ)Mott = W₂ + 2W₁tan²(θ/2)`, defines `W₁(q²,ν)`, `W₂(q²,ν)`, `R=σS/σT` (the paper's longitudinal/transverse notation), and says the small-angle data cannot determine `W₁` and `W₂` separately. Printed p. 4 treats `R=0` and `R=∞` as extreme assumptions. The abstract's conservative claim is transverse dominance with `νW₂` a function of `ω=2Mν/q²` within experimental errors for `q²>1 (GeV/c)²` and `ω>4`. Printed p. 5 explicitly says the longitudinal-dominance case does **not** follow a universal curve and `νW₂` increases with `q²` at fixed `2Mν/q²`. The reader translates the old paper's positive `q²` into modern positive `Q²`; no historical plot, fit coefficient, or dataset is reproduced.
- **SLAC institutional history:** “The hunting of the quarks” records the 1967 hydrogen/deuterium program, switch to inelastic scattering, and hard-grain interpretation. It is retrospective orientation, not the primary numerical record.
- **PDG 2025 Structure Functions review, official PDF rendered and checked:** §18.1 printed p. 1 defines `ν=E−E′` in the nucleon rest frame, modern positive `Q²=−q²≈4EE′sin²(θ/2)`, `W²=M²+2Mν−Q²`, and the deep/inelastic conditions. §18.2.1 printed p. 5 describes quark-parton scaling at fixed `x`; §18.2.2 printed pp. 5–6 says hard-gluon radiation produces logarithmic scaling violations and `Q²` evolution.
- **PDG 2025 QCD review, official PDF rendered and checked:** §9.2.2 printed p. 6 describes the quark-parton model as a photon interacting with point-like free quarks, equivalently incoherent elastic scattering from individual proton constituents, before higher-order radiation and scale-dependent PDFs are included.

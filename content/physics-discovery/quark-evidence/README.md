## Passages actually read

- **Nobel Prize 1990 press release and illustrated presentation:** “The prizewinners’ contributions” gives the 4–21 GeV beams, spectrometer angles, measured scattered-electron energy, and unexpectedly frequent large-angle/large-energy-loss events. “The SLAC–MIT experiment” says the charge appeared concentrated in negligible-size components and that the parton interpretation was later identified with the quark model. The presentation's simplified collision image is not treated as a literal photograph.
- **Breidenbach et al., SLAC-PUB-650 (1969):** printed p. 2 states that the measured differential cross section was divided by the Mott cross section to remove the major photon-propagator momentum-transfer dependence, after which the deep continuum retained much weaker `q²` dependence than elastic or resonance scattering. Printed pp. 2–5 define `W₁`, `W₂`, and `R=σL/σT`, state that the small-angle data could not separate the two structure functions, and present `W₂` bounds under `R=0` and `R=∞`. The plotted universal-curve quantity in pp. 3–5 is **`νW₂`**, not bare `W₂`; its consistency statement is limited by range and measurement errors.

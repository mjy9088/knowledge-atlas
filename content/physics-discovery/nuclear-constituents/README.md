# Nuclear constituents episode

This episode keeps Rutherford scattering separate from proton and neutron identification. The proton account follows the AIP Cavendish section's 1919–1920 alpha-bombardment evidence and preserves Rutherford's constituent interpretation as tentative rather than claiming that a complete nitrogen-capture-to-oxygen reaction pathway was demonstrated in 1919. For the neutron it preserves Chadwick's equal-mass/equal-speed penetration comparison and defines the recoil equation only for an initially stationary target's maximum recoil in a head-on elastic collision at common incident speed. The two-target ratio gives about 0.89 proton masses from the reported historical speeds. Primary-source comparison, expert review, rights review, audio, and publication remain pending.

Three neutron scenes use original static schematics because their reasoning depends on simultaneous comparison rather than hidden state or serial reveal. `neutral` compares a same-mass, same-speed proton stopping qualitatively with deeply penetrating beryllium radiation while keeping the gamma alternative unresolved by penetration alone. `collision-model` isolates the idealized maximum-transfer setup: an unseen projectile of mass `M` and common incident speed `V`, an initially stationary target of mass `A`, and one aligned head-on elastic collision giving `u_max=2MV/(M+A)`. `recoil` places hydrogen (`A=1`) and nitrogen (`A=14`) together, retains the reported historical maximum recoil speeds, and works the ratio through `M=(14-r)/(r-1)≈0.89` proton-mass units. These are not apparatus reconstructions, neutron-track photographs, simulations, measured trajectories, quantified thickness diagrams, or scale drawings. `question`, `limit`, and `transfer` retain their static cards; the proton remains the only interactive scene.

## Source scope and review limits

The decisive AIP passage was reopened from the canonical URL on 2026-10-18 before implementing the process. The consulted section reports 1919–1920 alpha bombardment of nitrogen and other light elements, ejected protons identified in Rutherford's terminology as hydrogen atoms, scintillation detection, Rutherford's limited constituent interpretation, unresolved questions about the target and alpha particle, and later Blackett evidence.

The actual Nobel Lecture PDF was opened on 2026-10-19 and printed pp. 341–342 were visually read. Printed p. 341 identifies the visible tracks as charged recoil protons and nitrogen atoms set in motion by the radiation; it does not show an incoming neutron track. Printed p. 342 first supposes particles of mass `M` with maximum speed `V`, gives the hydrogen and nitrogen maximum-recoil relations, reports approximately `3.7×10⁹ cm/s` and `4.7×10⁸ cm/s`, and obtains the historical estimate `M=0.9`. The same page then conditions the penetration inference on comparing particles of the same mass and speed, concluding that the beryllium-radiation particle has very small charge and adopting zero charge as the simplest explanation. This supports a condition → charged-recoil observation → unseen-projectile inference sequence; it does not turn penetration alone into a decisive rejection of the gamma alternative or the historical estimate into a modern precision value.

Printed pp. 341–342 are file-viewer pages 3–4 of the consulted ten-page PDF. Equal mass and speed belong to the conditional particle comparison, not a claim that an experiment independently controlled an unknown neutron mass. The schematic arrows are qualitative, not a plotted speed scale or photographed incoming track.

The implementation independently authors its geometry and wording and imports no AIP or Nobel figure, quotation, data table, photograph, or code.

# Quark confinement: explanation scope

The article asks why removing a quark from a proton does not leave an ordinary isolated quark. It is a focused entry into QCD, not a survey of particle physics. Canonical Korean prose and claims live in `content/particle-physics/quark-confinement/`; the route is `/knowledge/quark-confinement/`.

## Explanation sequence

1. Establish proton, quark and the limitation of the three-valence-quark picture.
2. Introduce color charge as a quantum label, rather than visible color.
3. Explain that gluons also interact with gluons, without pretending this proves confinement.
4. Identify separately observed hadrons as color singlets, not classical RGB mixtures.
5. Discuss the energy cost of separating static heavy probes within the confining regime.
6. Explain pair creation and string breaking when dynamical quarks are included.
7. Connect hadronization and jets to what detectors actually measure.
8. Distinguish short-distance asymptotic freedom from freely extractable quarks.
9. Return to the question and separate qualitative explanation, evidence, computation and proof.

## Scientific limits

- The discussion concerns ordinary vacuum hadrons, not every QCD phase. High-temperature matter and quark–gluon plasma need their own conditions.
- The approximately linear static-probe potential is not an all-distance formula for a proton or for dynamical quarks. String breaking prevents treating a real QCD connection as an infinitely extensible classical rubber band.
- A singlet is a quantum state. Assigning three fixed colored beads is not its complete state or evidence that exactly three particles are present in a proton.
- Gluon self-interaction, asymptotic freedom and confinement are distinct statements. The perturbative high-momentum result is not a general proof of long-distance confinement.
- Hadronization is not numerically simulated here; no trajectory, event generator, string tension, transition time or quark-extraction threshold is calculated.
- Hadron masses, the Higgs mechanism and residual nuclear forces are separate questions. They are excluded rather than appended as unrelated facts.

### Verdict vocabulary and evidence shorthand

- **adequate**: the current scene substantially serves the proposed purpose, with nonblocking refinements still possible.
- **revise**: the scene renders and may be correct, but its learning chain, representation, or interaction does not yet supply adequate evidence of the proposed purpose.
- **blocking**: a concrete defect prevents inspection of required information or materially contradicts the scene purpose.
- `Observed D/N` means the exact ID was loaded and captured at desktop and narrow sizes with bounds measured. `Adapter observed` means the surface-level changed/supported, unsupported, reset, restoration, and navigation scenarios listed above were actually run. `Untested` names evidence this review does not claim.

Actionable catalog-level blockers:

1. **Scene/representation mismatch:** model-backed readers initialize one shared first preset for every scene. ZFC `extensionality` therefore visibly shows the membership-transfer exercise; introductory ZFC/CH scenes also open with a proof/metaproof tool before the text establishes why it matters.
2. **Narrow CH inspection failure:** all CH scenes truncate the current goal input (`Ind(CH,ZFC)` visibly becomes `Ind(CH,ZF…`), so the exact target cannot be inspected at 390×844.
3. **No diagnostic transfer in most articles:** changing inputs is possible, but the reader is rarely asked to predict, explain, or receive rationale about a changed condition. Controls prove responsiveness, not learning.
4. **Representation types collapse into one template:** syntactic proof, semantic countermodel, set relation, and metatheoretic dependency use nearly identical cards/arrows/sidebars. Their different evidential roles remain in small prose rather than in the visual grammar.
5. **Weekday-worlds is useful but not a gold standard:** it does well at concrete opening, gradual disclosure, explicit simplification, synchronized current-input narration, preserved edits, and scope caveats. It fails through 17-scene sprawl, dense proof/system detours, small annotations, inefficient empty panels, weak elicitation of reasoning, and a conclusion that still carries much of the editor instead of delivering a clean payoff.

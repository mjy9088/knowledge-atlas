# Vision and priorities

Knowledge Atlas is a web-based atlas for exploring science through connected explanations, visualizations, sound, and meaningful experiments. It should feel like browsing a rich encyclopedia: readers enter anywhere, follow their interests, and choose their own depth. Formal logic is the first pilot, not the platform's subject boundary. Future material includes natural sciences, computing, engineering, statistics, and connections across disciplines.

## Editorial principles

- Select work by knowledge structure, evidence availability, explanation gaps, and reusable models. Views, watch time, and click-through rates are not objectives for selecting scientific claims.
- Use maintained textbooks, scholarly works, encyclopedias, and datasets as attributed sources. External sources inform reviewed claims; ingestion does not automatically make a statement accepted.
- Distinguish established results, deductions within a model, observations, approximations, hypotheses, and unresolved questions. Preserve conditions and uncertainty across every representation.
- Let enjoyment arise from understanding, experimenting, and discovering connections. Do not manufacture surprises by distorting science.
- Offer background links without compulsory prerequisites, progress locks, or an imposed curriculum. A static, well-sourced explanation is valid when interaction adds little.

## Web source, video projection

Claims, models, explanation blocks, and scenes form the shared source. A video fixes one path through that source, with projection-specific narration, timing, captions, and emphasis. It must independently explain its promised topic, while preserving the web version's assumptions and limits. A separate video script must not become a competing source of scientific truth.

A YouTube video has two roles: it should be enjoyable and informative on its own, and it should invite interested viewers to the corresponding web page for further exploration and hands-on changes. The video must deliver its promised explanation without requiring a website visit. Show the web interaction naturally and provide a concise route to the page; do not substitute repeated promotion, withheld answers, or click-through targets for the explanatory goal.

When changing assumptions or variables helps understanding, show editable examples inside the explanation. Preserve reader edits across scenes; update both dialogue and diagrams or clearly invalidate them. Restore suggested examples only through an explicit action. Shared components should support related pages through validated model adapters.

Use the same fields, checkboxes, selected values, and visible changes in video projections where useful. This should suggest the web version's experimental nature without requiring repeated promotional narration. Discoverability remains a user-research question; add a short link or hint when needed. Dedicated laboratories are appropriate for comparisons or specialized manipulation that improves understanding. Constants, definitions, and fixed source conditions must remain distinguishable from editable variables.

## Sustainable production

The long-term workflow is source discovery and permitted collection → versioned evidence → scoped claims and models → explanations and scenes → validation and review → web and optional video → targeted feedback and revision. People and agents edit the same inspectable source. Regeneration must preserve accepted human decisions and surface conflicts as proposed diffs.

Source updates, scientific reassessment, and publication authorization are separate actions. Published versions should eventually lock dependencies and support corrections and rollback. This is a target architecture, not a claim that the entire workflow is implemented.

## Priorities and non-goals

Prioritize semantic accuracy and provenance; accessible independent exploration; preservation and reproducibility; sustainable production; then visual polish. Do not introduce a large graph database, complete professional editor, orchestration platform, or automated publication system before a small vertical slice justifies it. Astro, React, Remotion, TTS vendors, and deployment providers remain replaceable implementation choices.

The pilot validates a vertical path from evidence to explanation, interaction, projection, and constrained revision. Generalization requires examples with different challenges, such as physical units, measurement uncertainty, and biological processes.

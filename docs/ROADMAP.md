# Remaining work and completion criteria

This document tracks unimplemented work and unresolved review. Current behavior belongs in its owning specification and source. Completed implementation/verification history belongs to Git and task reports; do not append dated test counts here.

## Priorities

1. **Evidence and rights:** legally capture immutable source bytes/hashes; verify exact revisions and claim spans; resolve rights and repository licensing; obtain independent content review. Preserve pending statuses until performed.
2. **Content and reader quality:** resolve concrete value, coherence, reasoning, source, representation and interaction gaps wherever inspection finds them. Apply the same review to chaptered explanations and weekday/probability subjects rather than privileging initial examples or inheriting old manuscripts. The [open assessment](reviews/content-quality/README.md) is unresolved historical evidence, not approval of later revisions. Complete accessibility, reduced-motion, screen-reader and cross-browser review; review readability, reasoning transfer and interaction discoverability with people. A repaired example or green suite does not establish comprehension.
3. **Projection integration:** compare sampled web/video states across output profiles, strengthen repeatable render diagnostics, add approved narration/audio and pronunciation handling. Do not mark the original storyboard/event schema fully integrated merely because the focused episode renders.
4. **Feedback preservation:** extend the local studio's manuscript review/feedback workflow with rendered-slide/audio review evidence and accepted-edit conflict handling during regeneration. Keep agent review readiness separate from content/expert/rights/publication approval.
5. **Publication lifecycle:** approved dependency/rights manifests, explicit release authorization, deploy/rollback, correction notices, and source refresh candidates. Static build support does not complete this milestone.
6. **Scope discipline:** choose improvements and additional subjects by actual explanatory need, evidence and editorial authorization, not an unused taxonomy slot, a fixed pair of flagship topics or an article-count target. The initial examples do not limit the atlas's broader science scope. Introduce frameworks/databases/queues only when demonstrated needs justify them.

## Lifecycle targets

Proposed states are draft → candidate → automatically checked → review required/approved → published, with withdrawn/deprecated states for serious problems. Review evidence should name its scope rather than use one numerical confidence badge.

Source revision monitoring and external scientific reassessment are separate. A live link or unchanged source is not proof of current scientific validity. Compare versions, classify formatting/terminology/numerical/semantic/rights changes, and follow actual dependency edges. Conflicting sources remain explicit alternatives until reviewed. Failed retrieval retains the last successful version without claiming freshness.

Regeneration should produce a new candidate, preserve accepted patches, and offer a three-way diff when new evidence conflicts with a human decision. Partial invalidation must account for adjacent timing/caption changes. Shared-component changes require inspecting all affected content. Extend the [local studio](../tools/studio/README.md) with reproducible model/tool configuration, usage budgets, accepted-edit merge candidates and rendered evidence reviews before treating it as a general production orchestrator.

Before publication, lock the reviewed source/claim/model/scene/audio/projection versions and rights notices, verify the resulting media, and authorize that exact manifest. Keep prior versions for rollback and manage corrections for already distributed videos. Publication authorization and upload remain pending.

## Completion criteria

A task reports changed components and relevant IDs, actual test/demo evidence, known limitations, and pending review/publication. A milestone requires executable code and the corresponding review evidence; passing structural or browser tests alone cannot certify scientific correctness or rights clearance.

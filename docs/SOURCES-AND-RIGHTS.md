# Evidence, rights, and publication

Free access is not permission to translate, redistribute, adapt, monetize, collect automatically, or use material in generated media. Review source text, separately owned figures/audio/fonts/data/code, and service/API conditions individually. This document records project policy, not new legal advice or source/license verification.

## Evidence records

Each substantive claim needs a stable ID, a precise statement, scope and assumptions, exact source locators, and an explicit review/uncertainty state. Source records need authorship, title, canonical URL, retrieval date, provider revision where known, and rights notes. A content hash is recorded only when the actual bytes have been captured and checked. Never invent revisions, quotations, licenses, or approval results.

For PDFs, distinguish the printed page number from the file viewer's page index when they differ, and record the displayed edition. Verify the actual theorem or passage on that page rather than inferring its location from a neighboring heading or remembered citation.

Encyclopedias and knowledge datasets can suggest terminology and links; a mention is not a verified prerequisite, identity mapping, or derivation. New analogies and inferred relationships are proposals with their own provenance. Conflicting evidence remains visible rather than being silently merged.

## Consultation versus reuse

Reading and citing a reference is recorded separately from including third-party material. Independently authored explanations and independently implemented models do not imply a permission request to every cited source owner. Source metadata may state `reference-only; third-party reuse not proposed` with `externalMaterialIncluded: false`. If prose, figures, datasets, fonts or code are later imported or adapted, review that specific use under the following process. Do not label reference consultation itself as awaiting permission.

## Reuse review

1. Record the exact source/version, ownership, notice, intended use, and affected spans/assets.
2. Check the applicable license and collection/service conditions for that version and intended use. Do not infer media rights from a site's general license.
3. Review adaptations, translations, figures, music, fonts, and software separately. Original wording or a new recording does not automatically eliminate underlying rights.
4. Produce required attribution, links, license/modification notices, and contributor information without implying endorsement.
5. Hold public release while rights or publication approvals remain unresolved. Changing an external source must not silently replace an already reviewed version with a newly restricted one.

License names alone do not establish compatibility: check attribution, share-alike, noncommercial, no-derivatives, and jurisdiction-specific limits against the actual intended use. A repository without a license does not grant an open-source license. The project's own license is still undecided and separate from every external source license.

## Audio, video, and future releases

Check TTS provider terms, commercial use, account permissions, voice imitation, and underlying audio rights before adding narration. A screenshot or video recording may retain the rights obligations of its displayed material. Remotion, if adopted, requires review of its actual license rather than an assumed MIT license.

A future approved publication manifest must lock source/license snapshots, claims/models, scenes, captions/audio, projection, attribution, and review records. Publish that exact version only with explicit authorization. Keep correction/withdrawal notices and prior manifests for rollback; a replaced web page does not automatically correct a distributed video.

# Reader experience

## Information and navigation

A concept identifies a subject; an article explains one or more subjects; a typed relation connects them in context; a source supplies evidence; a scene presents a state; a projection fixes a path through scenes. These entities are not necessarily one-to-one. External article IDs and matching names do not establish concept identity.

Readers can enter through a link or knowledge index, read an explanation, change meaningful conditions, inspect evidence, and continue to related topics. Links explain their relationship without imposing a course. Search, multilingual aliases, filtering, and richer graph browsing are future features; a large force-directed graph is not required as the default interface.

Use an authored short title where supplied for catalog cards and the visible article heading, while preserving the full title in document/share-title metadata and accessible title attributes. Existing articles fall back to their full titles. The catalog remains text-only. Optional vector cover artwork belongs to the title slide, separately from any future thumbnail design; keep the actual HTML heading, meaningful image alternative, and readable topic context. The weekday cover recreates the meme's challenge heading and 20-second motif as editorial illustration, not an IQ assessment or an enforced timer. See [ADR 0020](adr/0020-article-titles-and-vector-artwork.md).

## Interactive reading contract

- Present a diagram and dialogue with previous/next controls, direct scene selection, and keyboard navigation. Reading must not require waiting for playback or clicking an extra “expand inference” action.
- Show a skip action only when an authored destination advances beyond ordinary Next. Hide the button and skip-scope text when the destination is absent, adjacent or at the end. Name the skipped topic in the button, and disclose the remaining scope/count and destination nearby; preserve these details after input edits and reset. Catalog article scenes currently have no multi-scene skip destination. The focused lesson may skip the remaining scenes of a chapter.
- Proposed premises and goals are editable starting examples. Reader edits survive navigation. Diagrams, proof rows, dialogue, and evaluation status must follow the same input and model result. Scene titles retain their topic identity. Unsupported or incomplete input clears stale results; inapplicable explanation content is disabled and replaced by a separate status message, rather than renaming the scene. Input editing and navigation remain available, and recovery re-enables the content. Do not silently change a reader's goal to make an evaluation succeed.
- A bounded search failing to find a proof does not establish falsity, impossibility, consistency, or independence. A model that finds an actual countervaluation may describe that stronger result within its stated semantics.
- Reuse the editor, state adapter, and inference visualizer. Different subjects select different verified rule adapters; they must not pretend to use a universal proof engine.
- Preserve static readable content where implemented, and provide accessible controls, captions/transcripts, reduced-motion behavior, and meaningful alternatives. Automated geometry checks do not replace a full accessibility review.

## Viewport contract

Enhanced reading scenes must fit CSS viewports of 1920×1080, 1920×940, and 1920×900. The smaller heights are representative available browser areas, not universal measurements of address bars or taskbars. Test 390×844 separately for narrow reading. The weekday landscape/YouTube baseline also requires 1920×1080 physical output at 200% scale: 960×540 CSS pixels captured at DPR 2. Both input and reasoning remain simultaneously visible at this compact desktop size; this is not the narrow-screen tab layout. Use dynamic viewport height rather than assuming physical screen dimensions.

Normal scene examples must show their title, input, diagram, proof list, dialogue, and navigation without page scrolling or internal list/text scrolling. Verify actual content dimensions, element bounds, and unobscured controls; `overflow:hidden` alone does not satisfy the contract. Keep chrome to small logo/menu controls rather than large top and bottom bars.

The weekday-world desktop workbench should use at least 90% of a supported 1920px viewport, with primary selectors at least 13px and 34px tall. Stack Original, Symbols and Selection on the left, keeping Original and Symbols simultaneously visible and allowing only Selection to scroll. All three panes have white backgrounds and internal headings. Use compact colored original-language terms as triggers for the four world dropdowns; the target weekday also opens its own dropdown in the sentence. Keep full labels, keyboard selection, world creation and nested alignment in the captured DOM popovers. The Selection pane has a single internal `선택:` heading in the same row as A/B/C buttons; do not repeat the action word on each button or duplicate that heading. Put the alethic-mode checkbox inside Symbols; it switches the original sentence between simplified material and wish/counterfactual translations and updates the matching right-hand visualization. General UI uses Reality and Assumption 1–3; explicit formulas retain w₀–w₃. World identity keeps fixed pale green/blue/yellow/red colors across both naming systems, regardless of deletion order. Evaluation-point thickness, accessibility dashes and textual truth values remain distinct from identity color. Inspect the static build with standalone Playwright; an in-app-browser demonstration is not the verification method.

The weekday article opts into a dedicated title slide. Show its full article title there, keeping only compact navigation and the current scene heading during content reading. Root entry and reset show the title; direct scene hashes bypass it, and the title menu entry returns to it without losing edits. Preserve title/content presentation in saved state independently of the nine authored content scenes.

The weekday reader has no persistent top or bottom bar. Global navigation is an overlay revealed by hovering at the top edge or focusing its links with the keyboard; revealing it must not shift content. Place contents/evidence at the top of a vertical action rail on the explanation area's right edge, followed immediately by Previous and Next. Put knowledge-index and evidence/scope links inside the contents/evidence menu at every scene, without duplicate continuation links in the rail. Keep navigation keyboard help only in the Previous/Next button tooltips and accessible shortcut metadata, not as a visible hint row. Keep Next at identical viewport bounds on title, every content scene, the final state and restored inputs. The three rail actions form a compact stack without hint/link rows. The explanation height follows prose readability rather than the auxiliary links. Keep the scene title on the left and progress (for example, 1 / 9) at the right end of the same heading row. Align the prose toward the top with only 4–6 CSS pixels of vertical padding, rather than centering it inside excess height. Reserve 104 CSS pixels for wide desktop, 96 for the 200% landscape profile, and 136 on narrow screens, retaining invariant navigation bounds and readable text. The workbench uses the recovered space. The contents menu must stay inside the viewport at every supported scale; its close button stays reachable after scrolling. Support explicit close, Escape and outside click, preserving keyboard focus for explicit dismissal. Other article readers retain their existing navigation layout. See [ADR 0023](adr/0023-focus-reader-and-scaled-capture.md).

Display W from the current world's configuration in the symbol views. Removing a world removes its existing-world options, annotations, cards, graph node, matrix row/column and incident edges. References/evaluation at that world move to w₀ as disclosed in the configuration dialog. Preserve the removal through scene navigation and saved-state restoration. An absent world may appear only as an explicit creation action in the separate "Add new world" option group; selecting it, an example reset or a preset may restore that world.

The weekday reader has no Reality-today input or per-world weekday settings. The sentence target is an explicit premise edited only in the original sentence; all displayed calendar days come from the satisfying assignments of Γ. Show a unique weekday only if all assignments agree, otherwise show undetermined or no solution. Adding an unlinked world or changing accessibility cannot supply a weekday. Legacy saved sample days remain validated for restoration but never appear as inferred facts. See [ADR 0022](adr/0022-inferred-calendar-and-inline-contexts.md).

All four reference dropdowns and the evaluation-world dropdown offer unused IDs through explicit creation options. Selecting one atomically adds that world and assigns the reference/evaluation point, using the same stable identity, self-loop and edge from the previous evaluation point as the Add World button. Creation options remain available under the accessibility filter. Existing-world labels identify the numbered expressions currently referencing that world, excluding the dropdown's own expression; evaluation choices list all four. Refresh every list after edits, deletion, navigation and restoration, and keep world identity colors stable.

Explicit exceptions: long user-created premise collections, unusually long expressions/results, and opened evidence/navigation panels may scroll within their own bounded areas. These must preserve access to goal editing and scene navigation. Opened world/access-configuration dialogs and the weekday Selection panel may also scroll within their viewport-bound areas. Verify every Selection control can be scrolled into view and activated, while the original sentence, symbols, reset and scene navigation remain outside that scroll area. The weekday comparison shows input and reasoning simultaneously: two columns on desktop and at the 200% landscape baseline, stacked on narrow screens. There are no Input/Reasoning tabs. Retain legacy panel state solely for saved-state compatibility. The full static document without enhancement and the narrow knowledge index may scroll normally.

Related knowledge and detailed evidence/scope belong in an on-demand panel, with small continuation links on the final scene. Essential assumptions, model limits, and result status must remain visible in the explanation itself. The 200% landscape explanation is a compact 96 CSS-pixel area (192 physical pixels), with source/prose text at least 24 output pixels. Review open dropdowns and full frames at the actual video dimensions; this does not establish viewer comprehension. See [ADR 0024](adr/0024-continuous-reader-and-inline-choices.md).

## Weekday modal-logic introduction

The weekday article aims at enjoyment and recognition that modal logic exists, rather than a comprehensive formal course or a literacy/IQ assessment. Familiarity with classical logic is recommended, but actual readers may arrive without it. Explain only the concepts needed to follow the example; do not introduce premise-set notation (such as Γ), model-set names or entailment symbols in the primary display merely to describe the solver.

Begin with ordinary weekday reasoning, compare the two explicitly chosen reference readings, then name the hypothetical situations as possible worlds. Introduce possibility/necessity briefly, compare a material-conditional simplification and the authored wish/counterfactual reading together, and continue with the latter. The comparison is not an equivalence claim. Preserve the distinction between a counterfactual and strict implication, between wishes and alethic possibility, and between a translated statement and the additional conditions used for the calendar calculation. Keep the contradiction/explosion connection brief and available as related knowledge.

Authored scene defaults move from nonmodal to modal after the comparison. A reader's explicit mode choice persists through navigation; explicit scene reset resumes the authored mode. Calendar edits remain preserved. The bridge scene displays both readings simultaneously, and direct links use the appropriate stage without requiring earlier clicks. See [ADR 0025](adr/0025-weekday-modal-introduction.md).

## Question-led explanations

New introductory explanations should name a concrete question and an observable learning outcome before introducing terminology. Let readers observe, compare, explain, and try a changed condition. Optional predictions and answer reveals must not block navigation or require a correct answer. This is an authoring pattern, not a mandatory sequence for every subject: proofs may need explicit justifications, observational topics need uncertainty, and prose-only explanations remain valid.

Each interaction must make clear what can be changed, what to look for, and how the result bears on the question. Keep essential assumptions and representation limits visible. Advanced details may be optional, but the primary path must independently answer its opening question. Customized inputs require scene-specific explanation of the current result; preserve reader edits through navigation and restore authored examples only by an explicit action.

Use transfer questions to invite application to a changed case. A revealed explanation is feedback, not proof that a person understood. Automated semantic, restoration, and layout checks are implementation evidence; learning quality still needs observation with intended readers. Do not infer understanding from clicks, time spent, or a passing geometry test.

## Reading, animation, and production

The interactive reader is self-paced and should not resemble a video player. Selected proof steps and restored state remain deterministic. Retired comparison/laboratory prototypes are not part of current navigation.

Mobile web reading and portrait projection are separate layouts. The local renderer supports 1080×1440 (3:4), superseding the earlier 9:16 proposal. Place the title and optional short explanation above/below the central reasoning; do not move essential conditions into optional text. The former standalone portrait and three-iframe comparison pages are retired; the local media tool provides production frame preview.

Short explanations have a project maximum of 180 seconds, not a duration target. The focused pilot is 134 seconds. Do not add filler or compress a long proof merely to fit that maximum. Production tools and MP4 conversion are separate from the reader-facing static site; see [Development](DEVELOPMENT.md).

## Evidence and feedback

Where authored text/formula alignment is available, hover or keyboard focus must reveal the entire nested root-to-selected path on both representations. Do not attach native hover tooltips to the highlighted fragments: the direct emphasis is the feedback. Introduce the hover interaction briefly in the explanation. Keep the path available to assistive technology; the weekday reader does not reserve a visible instruction/breadcrumb row. Repeated terms with different semantic roles remain distinct. Preserve identity colors and layout geometry; clear stale emphasis when inputs change. Explicitly distinguish a partial formalization from a complete translation. The weekday reader maps its conditional connective to an explicitly simplified A→B reading, separately from the solver's simultaneous Γ constraints. Enabling modality replaces the primary formula with the mapped wish/counterfactual reading; it does not append an independent atomic-P comparison. Keep A/B definitions on separate lines and the question beside the active formula. Omit the duplicate left Γ row. Mark calendar deductions as additional simultaneous calendar conditions and do not fabricate a counterfactual truth value without a similarity model. Keep the modality checkbox on the Symbols heading row. The shared alignment contract is [ADR 0019](adr/0019-structural-expression-alignment.md); source-translation semantics are defined in [ADR 0021](adr/0021-source-modal-translation.md).

Evidence panels expose claim IDs, exact scopes, assumptions, source locators, review states, and rights information. Source synchronization dates and content review dates must be distinguishable. Reader suggestions enter review rather than automatically changing scientific content. Accepted corrections should eventually link to affected web and video versions.

Durable feedback decisions live in this document, the [Vision](VISION.md), the [Architecture](ARCHITECTURE.md), or a scoped [ADR](adr/README.md). Task-local changes and their validation remain in Git. A separate structured feedback artifact is justified only by an unresolved review or an actual workflow consumer; do not produce one JSON file per conversation turn.
